# Professional Website Development Skill

## 1. Mission and Design Philosophy

Build professional, modern, responsive, and production-ready websites that communicate trust, clarity, and credibility.

Every website must prioritize:

- Clear information architecture.
- Professional typography and readable content.
- Consistent visual identity.
- Simple, intentional layouts.
- Purposeful calls to action.
- Subtle, smooth animations.
- Mobile-first responsive design.
- Accessibility and usability.
- Functional navigation, forms, and interactive elements.
- Maintainable, secure, and well-structured code.

Avoid unnecessary visual complexity. A website should communicate the business's value rather than distract visitors with excessive decoration.

**Design rule:** Every color, animation, icon, image, and component must serve a clear purpose.

## 2. Color System

### Approved primary colors

Use a restrained palette based on these colors:

- White: `#FFFFFF`
- Black: `#111111`
- Brown: `#795548`
- Blue: `#2563EB`
- Light neutral: `#F5F5F5`
- Dark neutral: `#262626`
- Muted text: `#666666`
- Border: `#E5E7EB`

These are starting values, not mandatory brand colors. Adjust shades when necessary to meet contrast requirements or match an existing brand.

### Color selection rules

1. Choose one primary brand color: blue or brown, unless the client's established branding requires another color.
2. Use white and neutral colors for backgrounds, content surfaces, and spacing.
3. Use black or dark neutral colors for primary text.
4. Use the selected brand color consistently for primary buttons, links, focus indicators, and important interactive elements.
5. Use brown as the primary color when the brand identity calls for brown.
6. Use blue as the primary color when the brand identity calls for blue.
7. Do not mix blue and brown as competing primary colors without a clear brand reason.
8. Use light backgrounds and restrained borders to distinguish sections.
9. Maintain readable text contrast across every background.
10. Avoid introducing additional colors without a specific design requirement.

### Button color logic

The primary button color must follow the website's primary brand color.

- Blue-primary website → blue primary buttons.
- Brown-primary website → brown primary buttons.
- White-and-black website → choose a suitable primary accent if needed; otherwise use black or another established brand color.

Use white text on colored primary buttons when contrast is sufficient.

Secondary buttons may use transparent backgrounds, neutral borders, or restrained dark styling.

Buttons must have visible hover, keyboard-focus, active, and disabled states where applicable.

Do not randomly change button colors between sections.

## 3. Typography and Content

Typography must be professional, consistent, and readable.

### Typography rules

- Use a maximum of two font families unless the brand requires more.
- Prefer clean sans-serif fonts for modern business websites.
- Establish a consistent hierarchy for headings, subheadings, body text, captions, and buttons.
- Use responsive font sizes.
- Maintain comfortable line height and paragraph spacing.
- Avoid excessively large headings that overwhelm the page.
- Avoid overly small body text.
- Use bold text selectively to emphasize important information.

### Writing rules

Website content must be clear, professional, specific, and easy to understand.

- Explain what the business does, who it serves, and what value it provides.
- Use paragraphs where detailed explanations are necessary.
- Use concise headings and subheadings to organize information.
- Use bullet lists for services, benefits, features, and processes where appropriate.
- Include calls to action that match the section's purpose.
- Avoid filler text, repetitive statements, exaggerated claims, and meaningless marketing language.
- Never invent testimonials, client names, certifications, statistics, or company achievements.
- Use real business information supplied by the client.
- If information is unavailable, use clearly identified placeholders during development.

Content should explain the business rather than merely decorate the interface.

## 4. Website Architecture

A standard business website should support the following pages:

1. Home
2. About
3. Services
4. Contact
5. Admin dashboard, when the project requires content or business management.

Additional pages should be created when the business needs them, such as pricing, projects, portfolio, products, blog, FAQs, or individual service-detail pages.

### Global navigation

Create a consistent header containing:

- Business logo or name.
- Main navigation links.
- A prominent call-to-action button when appropriate.
- A mobile navigation menu for smaller screens.

The header must work across all public pages.

Keep navigation labels simple and predictable. Avoid unnecessary dropdowns.

The header may be sticky when doing so improves navigation without consuming excessive screen space.

### Global footer

Include relevant information such as:

- Business name and a short description.
- Links to important pages.
- Contact information.
- Location, when relevant.
- Social media links, when provided.
- Copyright information.
- Privacy policy and other legal links when required.

Do not add empty social links or fictional contact information.

## 5. Home Page

The home page is the primary entry point and must communicate the business's value quickly.

### 5.1 Hero section

The hero section must use a centered composition by default.

Include:

- A concise headline.
- A short supporting description.
- An animated text element that changes between relevant messages.
- One primary call-to-action button.
- An optional secondary call-to-action.
- An appropriate visual, when it contributes to the brand and message.

The text and actions should be centered horizontally and visually balanced.

### Animated hero text

Create a rotating text sequence that communicates different aspects of the business.

Example:

**Headline:** Building better solutions for your business.

**Animated phrases:**
- Digital experiences that engage.
- Services designed around your needs.
- Solutions built for long-term growth.

Use a smooth fade-in/fade-out transition or a subtle slide transition.

Animation requirements:

- Change phrases automatically at a reasonable interval.
- Keep the transition smooth and professional.
- Avoid rapid flashing or distracting movement.
- Prevent layout shifts when phrases have different lengths.
- Ensure text remains readable during transitions.
- Pause or reduce motion when appropriate for accessibility.
- Provide a useful static fallback when animation is unavailable.

The phrases must reflect the actual business and must not make unsupported claims.

### Hero CTA buttons

Use action-oriented labels appropriate to the business, such as:

- Explore Our Services
- Contact Us
- Request a Consultation
- View Our Work
- Get Started

Every button must navigate to a valid destination or trigger a working action.

### 5.2 Home page sections

Build the home page as a structured overview of the entire business.

Select relevant sections from the following:

1. Hero.
2. Business introduction.
3. Featured services.
4. Why choose us.
5. How the business works.
6. Featured projects, products, or portfolio.
7. Business statistics, only when verified.
8. Testimonials, only when authentic.
9. Frequently asked questions, when useful.
10. Contact or conversion section.
11. Footer.

Not every website needs every section. Choose sections based on the business model and user journey.

### Section design rules

Each section should contain:

- A clear heading.
- A concise introduction where necessary.
- Relevant content, images, or supporting information.
- A call to action when a logical next step exists.

Some sections should use short paragraphs, while others may use cards, lists, images, or structured content.

Avoid making every section look identical. Create visual variety through layout, spacing, typography, and background changes rather than unnecessary colors and effects.

### Links to internal pages

The home page must direct visitors to relevant detailed pages.

For example:

- Featured services → Services page.
- Company introduction → About page.
- Featured projects → Portfolio page.
- Contact CTA → Contact page.

Use descriptive link labels. Avoid buttons that do not lead anywhere.

## 6. About Page

The About page must explain the business's identity, purpose, and credibility.

Use relevant sections such as:

- Company introduction.
- Mission and vision.
- Values.
- History or background.
- Team information, when available.
- Approach or working methodology.
- Relevant achievements or credentials, when verified.
- CTA directing visitors to services or contact.

Write complete, meaningful paragraphs rather than relying entirely on cards and short labels.

Use authentic images when available. Avoid generic AI-generated imagery that does not represent the actual business.

The page should answer three questions:

1. Who is the business?
2. What does it stand for?
3. Why should a potential customer trust it?

Do not invent company history or credentials.

---

## 7. Services Page

The Services page must make the business's offerings easy to understand.

### Services overview

Include:

- A clear page introduction.
- A summary of the services offered.
- A detailed explanation of each major service.
- Relevant deliverables, outcomes, or benefits.
- A clear next step for interested visitors.

### Individual service sections

Each service should include, where applicable:

- Service name.
- Short description.
- The problem it addresses.
- What the service includes.
- Expected deliverables or outcomes.
- A CTA linking to a dedicated service page or contact form.

Use cards for concise service summaries and longer sections for detailed explanations.

Create separate service-detail pages when offerings require substantial explanation.

Do not force every service into a card if paragraphs or a structured list communicate the information more effectively.

---

## 8. Contact Page

The Contact page must make contacting the business straightforward.

Include relevant elements such as:

- Contact form.
- Email address.
- Phone number.
- Business location or address.
- Business hours.
- Map, when useful and supported.
- Social links, when provided.

### Contact form

Use appropriate fields, such as:

- Full name.
- Email address.
- Subject, when needed.
- Message.

Add additional fields only when the business requires them.

Form requirements:

- Validate input on both client and server where applicable.
- Display clear validation errors.
- Show submission progress.
- Display success and failure messages.
- Prevent accidental duplicate submissions.
- Protect against spam and malicious input.
- Ensure submitted messages reach the intended destination.
- Never display a success message when submission has failed.

Provide clear labels for all fields. Do not rely exclusively on placeholder text.

### Contact information

Use recognizable icons sparingly for email, phone, location, and similar contact details.

Icons must have clear meanings and accessible labels where necessary.

Never invent email addresses, phone numbers, locations, or map coordinates.

## 9. Admin Dashboard

An admin dashboard is required only when the project needs administrative functionality.

It must be separate from the public-facing website while maintaining consistent branding.

### Typical dashboard features

Implement only the features relevant to the business:

- Secure authentication.
- Dashboard overview.
- Content management.
- Service management.
- Image and media management.
- Contact-message management.
- Product or project management, when applicable.
- User management, when required.
- Site settings.
- Logout.

### Admin UI requirements

- Use a clear sidebar or compact navigation.
- Organize features into predictable sections.
- Provide tables, forms, filters, and search where useful.
- Include loading, empty, error, and success states.
- Support responsive layouts.
- Make destructive actions explicit and require confirmation when appropriate.
- Provide clear feedback after successful changes.

Do not add decorative dashboard widgets that have no meaningful data or functionality.

### Admin security

- Enforce authentication on protected routes and APIs.
- Verify authorization on the server for every privileged operation.
- Never rely on hidden buttons or client-side route checks as the only security mechanism.
- Validate and authorize all data mutations.
- Store credentials and secrets in appropriate server-side environment variables.
- Never expose database credentials, private API keys, or administrative secrets in client-side code.
- Use secure session handling and appropriate protections against common web vulnerabilities.
- Apply least-privilege access.
- Avoid exposing sensitive customer information unnecessarily.

A visually hidden admin page is not a secure admin system.

## 10. Icons and Visual Elements

Use icons selectively.

Appropriate uses include:

- Email.
- Phone.
- Location.
- Navigation.
- Search.
- Menu controls.
- Social media links.
- Functional dashboard actions.

### Icon rules

- Prefer a consistent icon library, such as Lucide, when appropriate for the chosen stack.
- Use consistent icon sizes and stroke weights.
- Use familiar symbols with recognizable meanings.
- Pair unfamiliar icons with text labels.
- Provide accessible labels for icon-only controls.
- Avoid unnecessary icons beside every heading or paragraph.

Do not fill the interface with decorative AI-generated icons, random symbols, glowing shapes, or excessive floating elements.

Avoid using AI-themed badges, sparkle symbols, robot graphics, and artificial intelligence labels unless the actual business and design brief specifically require them.

Use real photographs, product images, diagrams, and brand assets where they communicate useful information.

## 11. Backgrounds, Gradients, and Decorative Effects

Keep backgrounds simple.

Preferred backgrounds:

- White.
- Black or dark neutral.
- Light gray.
- Restrained brand-colored surfaces.

Use alternating section backgrounds when they improve content separation.

### Gradient policy

Do not use gradients by default.

Avoid gradients added merely to make a website look futuristic, fashionable, or artificially sophisticated.

Only use a gradient when it has a clear brand or visual communication purpose and is consistent with the design brief.

Avoid unnecessary:

- Glassmorphism.
- Excessive shadows.
- Glowing borders.
- Blurred decorative circles.
- Floating abstract shapes.
- Neon highlights.
- Decorative background patterns.
- Excessive rounded cards.

Use borders, whitespace, typography, and layout to create hierarchy.

Shadows and rounded corners are acceptable when restrained and consistent.

## 12. Animation and Motion Design

Animations should make the interface easier to follow without distracting visitors.

### Approved animation patterns

**Fade in:** Elements gradually become visible.

**Fade out:** Elements gradually disappear when changing states or content.

**Slide in:** Elements move a short distance into view.

**Scroll-triggered reveal:** Sections or elements animate as they enter the viewport.

**Text transition:** Hero phrases change using fade or subtle sliding effects.

**Hover transitions:** Buttons, links, cards, and interactive elements respond smoothly to user interaction.

### Motion rules

- Use short, controlled transitions.
- Keep animations consistent throughout the website.
- Animate only elements that benefit from motion.
- Use scroll-triggered animations sparingly.
- Avoid animating every paragraph, card, and icon independently.
- Prevent content from remaining invisible when JavaScript fails.
- Ensure animations do not interfere with navigation or interaction.
- Avoid excessive parallax, bouncing, spinning, and continuous movement.
- Respect the user's `prefers-reduced-motion` setting.
- Maintain readability and avoid layout shifts.

A reasonable starting point is approximately 200–400 ms for simple UI transitions. Scroll reveals may use slightly longer durations when necessary.

Use CSS transitions and keyframes for simple effects. Introduce an animation library only when the project's requirements justify it.

Animations must degrade gracefully when unsupported.

## 13. Responsive Design

Every website must work on mobile phones, tablets, laptops, and desktop monitors.

Use a mobile-first implementation strategy.

### Responsive requirements

- Use flexible layouts and fluid sizing.
- Use CSS Grid and Flexbox where appropriate.
- Avoid fixed widths that cause horizontal scrolling.
- Ensure navigation adapts to smaller screens.
- Make buttons and interactive controls easy to operate on touchscreens.
- Ensure forms remain usable on mobile.
- Scale typography appropriately.
- Maintain consistent spacing across screen sizes.
- Ensure images use appropriate sizing and cropping.
- Test long headings, long buttons, and different content lengths.
- Prevent overlapping text and controls.

Do not simply shrink the desktop layout. Adapt the information hierarchy and component arrangement to the available space.

Test at narrow mobile widths, tablet widths, and large desktop widths.

## 14. Accessibility and Usability

Accessibility is part of the implementation, not a final decoration.

Requirements:

- Use semantic HTML.
- Maintain a logical heading hierarchy.
- Provide descriptive labels for form fields.
- Support keyboard navigation.
- Display visible keyboard focus.
- Provide meaningful alternative text for informative images.
- Use empty alternative text for purely decorative images where appropriate.
- Maintain sufficient color contrast.
- Avoid communicating important states through color alone.
- Ensure dialogs and menus support appropriate keyboard behavior.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Make error messages understandable.
- Use accessible names for icon-only buttons.

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA compliance where applicable.

Do not remove native accessibility behavior without providing an equivalent alternative.

---

## 15. Images and Media

Images must support the business's message.

Use authentic assets supplied by the client whenever possible.

### Image rules

- Use appropriate aspect ratios.
- Avoid distorted or stretched images.
- Optimize file size and loading performance.
- Use responsive images when appropriate.
- Provide meaningful alternative text.
- Use lazy loading for suitable below-the-fold images.
- Prioritize important above-the-fold assets.
- Avoid unnecessary autoplay video.
- Provide a sensible fallback when an image is missing.
- Do not invent client logos, product photos, team photographs, or testimonials.

Do not use random stock photography merely to fill empty space.

## 16. Component Architecture and Code Quality

Build reusable components instead of duplicating the same interface throughout the application.

Typical shared components include:

- Header.
- Footer.
- Button.
- Section heading.
- Service card.
- Contact form.
- Input field.
- Modal.
- Navigation menu.
- Loading indicator.
- Error message.
- Admin sidebar.

### Engineering rules

- Follow the existing project's conventions when they are sound.
- Inspect the repository before modifying files.
- Preserve working functionality.
- Use meaningful file and component names.
- Separate UI components from business logic where appropriate.
- Use typed interfaces and validation where supported.
- Avoid duplicated code.
- Avoid oversized components that mix unrelated responsibilities.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies.
- Remove unused imports and dead code.
- Handle loading, errors, and empty states explicitly.
- Keep configuration separate from application logic.
- Never hardcode secrets.
- Use environment variables for environment-specific configuration.
- Document important setup and deployment requirements.

Do not rewrite the entire project simply to introduce a preferred framework or architecture.

## 17. Technology Stack

Choose the stack according to the project's requirements rather than forcing every project into one technology.

### Suggested default stack

For a modern business website:

- Frontend: Next.js with React and TypeScript.
- Styling: Tailwind CSS or well-structured CSS.
- Icons: Lucide or another consistent icon library.
- Animation: CSS transitions and keyframes; Motion when justified.
- Forms: validated client-side forms with server-side validation.
- Backend: Next.js server functionality or a separate API when necessary.
- Database: PostgreSQL or another suitable database when persistent data is required.
- Authentication: a proven authentication solution appropriate to the application.
- Hosting: Vercel or another platform compatible with the selected stack.
- Version control: Git and GitHub.

These are defaults, not mandatory dependencies.

For simple static websites, avoid introducing a database or backend without a real requirement.

For websites with administrative content management, persistent records, authentication, or user submissions, choose a suitable backend architecture.

Never claim that a frontend-only demonstration is a production-ready full-stack application.

## 18. Performance and SEO

Every public website should be optimized for discoverability and performance.

### Performance

- Optimize image dimensions and formats.
- Avoid unnecessarily large JavaScript bundles.
- Load nonessential resources only when needed.
- Minimize layout shifts.
- Avoid expensive animation effects.
- Use server rendering or static generation where appropriate.
- Cache suitable assets and responses.
- Avoid redundant network requests.
- Monitor Core Web Vitals.

### SEO

- Give every public page a meaningful title and description.
- Use semantic headings.
- Implement descriptive URLs.
- Provide canonical URLs where appropriate.
- Add social sharing metadata.
- Use structured data only when applicable and accurate.
- Provide a sitemap and robots.txt when appropriate.
- Ensure important content is accessible to search engines.
- Use descriptive image alternative text.
- Link related pages internally.
- Avoid duplicate page titles and descriptions.

Do not promise search rankings or fabricate business information for SEO purposes.

## 19. Functional Requirements and Testing

A website is not complete merely because its pages look correct.

Verify that:

- Navigation links work.
- CTA buttons lead to valid destinations.
- Forms validate input.
- Form submissions reach the intended backend.
- Loading and error states display correctly.
- Mobile navigation opens and closes correctly.
- Animations do not hide content permanently.
- Images load correctly.
- Admin authentication and authorization work.
- Protected endpoints reject unauthorized requests.
- Database operations behave correctly when applicable.
- Empty states display correctly.
- Browser console errors are investigated.
- Build and type checks pass.
- The application works at relevant responsive breakpoints.

Do not use fake success messages or mock data in production without explicitly identifying the implementation as a demonstration.

If a feature cannot be completed because an external service or credential is missing, explain the dependency and provide a clear integration point.

## 20. Development Workflow

Follow this workflow for every website project.

### Phase 1: Understand the requirements

Identify:

- Business type.
- Target audience.
- Main conversion goal.
- Required pages.
- Required content.
- Brand assets and colors.
- Required integrations.
- Administrative functionality.
- Hosting and deployment requirements.

Do not invent missing business facts.

Ask for clarification when an unknown requirement materially affects architecture or implementation.

### Phase 2: Inspect the project

Before coding:

- Inspect the repository structure.
- Identify the existing framework and dependencies.
- Review the current pages and components.
- Inspect available assets.
- Identify existing backend integrations.
- Understand existing functionality that must be preserved.

For a new project, establish a clean and maintainable structure.

### Phase 3: Establish the design system

Define:

- Primary brand color.
- Neutral palette.
- Typography.
- Spacing scale.
- Content width.
- Button variants.
- Border and shadow conventions.
- Responsive breakpoints.
- Animation rules.

Reuse the same design tokens across the application.

### Phase 4: Implement the shared layout

Build the header, navigation, footer, buttons, typography, and reusable components.

Verify that the shared layout works across screen sizes.

### Phase 5: Build the pages

Implement the Home, About, Services, and Contact pages.

Build the admin dashboard when required.

Make the homepage a meaningful overview of the site's other sections.

### Phase 6: Add interactions and animations

Implement functional CTAs, navigation, forms, menus, and the selected animations.

Test keyboard interaction and reduced-motion behavior.

### Phase 7: Integrate data and backend functionality

Connect required APIs, databases, authentication, and content management.

Validate server-side inputs and authorization.

Clearly separate demonstration behavior from production functionality.

### Phase 8: Test and refine

Check visual consistency, responsiveness, accessibility, performance, errors, and functionality.

Fix issues before declaring the project complete.

### Phase 9: Deploy and document

Provide:

- Setup instructions.
- Required environment variables, without exposing secret values.
- Build and deployment instructions.
- Database setup instructions when applicable.
- A summary of implemented features.
- Any remaining limitations or external dependencies.

## 21. Final Design Quality Checklist

Before finishing any website, verify each category.

### Design

- [ ] White, black, brown, and blue are used within a coherent brand system.
- [ ] One primary accent color guides buttons and important actions.
- [ ] Typography is readable and consistent.
- [ ] Layouts use whitespace effectively.
- [ ] Gradients are absent unless explicitly justified.
- [ ] Decorative elements do not overwhelm content.
- [ ] Icons are limited to meaningful uses.
- [ ] AI-themed decorations and badges are not added without a real reason.

### Content and pages

- [ ] Home page has a centered hero with changing text and clear CTAs.
- [ ] The homepage links to relevant internal pages.
- [ ] About content communicates the business's identity.
- [ ] Services are explained clearly.
- [ ] Contact information is accurate.
- [ ] Forms provide clear validation and feedback.
- [ ] Admin functionality is implemented when required.

### Motion and responsiveness

- [ ] Fade and slide animations are subtle.
- [ ] Scroll-triggered effects are used selectively.
- [ ] Reduced-motion preferences are respected.
- [ ] No essential content depends on animation.
- [ ] Mobile, tablet, and desktop layouts work correctly.
- [ ] Interactive elements remain usable on touchscreens.

### Engineering

- [ ] Components are reusable and maintainable.
- [ ] Navigation and buttons work.
- [ ] Forms and backend integrations work as intended.
- [ ] Protected admin operations are secured.
- [ ] No secrets are exposed.
- [ ] Loading, error, and empty states are handled.
- [ ] Build and type checks pass.
- [ ] Performance and SEO fundamentals are implemented.
- [ ] Setup and deployment instructions are available.

---

## 22. Final Instruction

Always build the simplest implementation that satisfies the business requirements while maintaining professional design quality.

Do not add features, libraries, colors, animations, icons, gradients, or architectural complexity merely because they are fashionable.

Prioritize business clarity, user trust, usability, maintainability, security, and reliable functionality.

**The expected result is a professional website that looks deliberate, reads clearly, works correctly, and can be maintained and extended without unnecessary complexity.**
